# Abstract {.unnumbered .unlisted}

```{=openxml}
<w:p><w:pPr><w:jc w:val="center"/></w:pPr><w:r><w:rPr><w:b/><w:sz w:val="32"/></w:rPr><w:t>Contemporary Juristic Issues (Nawazil) Related to Educational Institutions: A Comparative Fiqh Study</w:t></w:r></w:p>
<w:p><w:pPr><w:jc w:val="both"/></w:pPr><w:r><w:t xml:space="preserve">This dissertation examines contemporary juristic issues (nawazil) arising in schools and universities, with special reference to Palestine. Twenty-seven issues are studied in a preliminary chapter and four chapters: rulings concerning teachers (gender mixing in education, teaching Islamic courses by unqualified staff, teachers' strikes, refusal to work after arbitrary transfer, Teachers' Day celebrations, the status of the institutional prayer room, receiving salaries through interest-based banks, and private use of institutional property); the teacher–student relationship (gifts, private tutoring, promoting failing students, disciplining students, confiscation, and handling tuition fees); rulings concerning students (breaking the Ramadan fast during exams, obedience to teachers, learning from non-Muslim teachers, parental help with homework, standing for the teacher, cheating, and female students travelling without a mahram); and students' relationship with institutional regulations (uniforms, hijab, compulsory canteen purchases, and tuition fees).</w:t></w:r></w:p>
<w:p><w:pPr><w:jc w:val="both"/></w:pPr><w:r><w:t xml:space="preserve">Following an inductive, analytical and comparative method, each issue is described, its point of disagreement defined, and it is classified under its classical juristic principles. The views of the four Sunni schools are presented from their authoritative works, together with contemporary scholars, fiqh academies, fatwa bodies, including Sheikh Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani and Sheikh Abd al-Qadim Zallum, documented from their works; evidences are discussed and the strongest view is preferred. Sources are documented according to the Egyptian citation system.</w:t></w:r></w:p>
<w:p><w:pPr><w:jc w:val="both"/></w:pPr><w:r><w:t xml:space="preserve">Key findings include: the employed teacher is a "special hireling" (ajir khass), on which most of his rulings rest; his hand over institutional and student funds is a hand of trust; he holds no original guardianship over students; separation of the sexes is the default in education; cheating and assisting it are prohibited in all examinations; corporal punishment is impermissible under current contracts and laws; public education is a right the state must provide free of charge; and certificates may not be withheld from students for their guardians' debts. The study recommends clear Sharia-compliant regulations for educational institutions and further research on e-learning issues.</w:t></w:r></w:p>
<w:p><w:r><w:rPr><w:b/></w:rPr><w:t xml:space="preserve">Keywords: </w:t></w:r><w:r><w:t>Nawazil, educational institutions, teacher, student, hire contract (ijarah), Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Palestine.</w:t></w:r></w:p>
```
